# The city we walk

## The problem

Mexico City was designed for the car. I don't mean it as a metaphor. Walk ten blocks and you'll see it: broken sidewalks, impossible crossings, footbridges that make you climb three floors to cross an avenue.

Why do we accept this? Because we got used to it. For decades we were told that progress meant second decks and ring roads.

According to the origin-destination survey, most trips in the city are made by public transport, on foot or by bike. The car is a minority. And yet the space, the street and the budget are almost all its own.

That is not a mobility policy. It is a privilege with good press.

## What to do

We don't need to invent anything. Other cities already did it.

Bogotá closes avenues on Sundays so people can walk. Paris took lanes from cars and turned them into bike paths. School streets cost little and they work.

What I propose is simple: start with the schools. Then the avenues downtown. Little by little, with data.

The city we walk is the city we live in. Everything else is traffic.
